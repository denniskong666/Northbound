# Northbound Narrative Design Specification

**Status:** Approved concept; written specification awaiting user review  
**Date:** 2026-08-13  
**Theme:** Design My Life  
**Genre:** Top-down narrative exploration game  
**Target playtime:** 45–60 minutes  
**Primary language:** English  
**Presentation:** Stylized 2D/2.5D gameplay with four cinematic playback slots; the relationship-dependent character slot has three source variants, for six produced video clips in total

## 1. Creative Intent

*Northbound* is a short, single-player coming-of-age game about five friends preparing to leave a declining industrial town. The player initially understands the trip north as the game's objective: earn money, repair an old station wagon, and leave together. Over five in-fiction days, the friends discover that the promise they made as children no longer fits all of them.

The game expresses the summit theme, **Design My Life**, without stating it directly in dialogue. Its central idea is:

> A life you designed once does not have to become a life you are trapped inside.

The player should finish the game understanding that agency includes both making plans and revising them when identity, relationships, or circumstances change. No ending is labeled good, bad, true, or false.

## 2. Experience Goals

By the end of one playthrough, the player should:

1. Genuinely want the five friends to leave together during the opening chapter.
2. Understand why Maya, Noah, and Leo begin to want different futures.
3. Sympathize with Elias even as his loyalty becomes controlling.
4. Hesitate during at least one conflicting mission.
5. Feel uncertain when control returns after Elias asks, “Are you coming?”
6. Choose a final direction through movement rather than a menu.
7. Continue considering the unchosen paths after the ending.

The experience is bittersweet but hopeful. It may include family pressure, financial stress, disagreement, and separation, but no combat, graphic violence, drugs, or explicit adult content.

## 3. Scope and Structure

The story uses a five-day countdown. The car becomes more complete as the friendship becomes less unified.

| Segment | Target time | Primary purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Prologue | 3–4 min | Establish the childhood promise and symbol of North |
| Chapter 1: The Plan | 9–11 min | Teach mechanics and make the shared plan desirable |
| Chapter 2: Cracks | 10–12 min | Reveal each friend's emerging direction |
| Chapter 3: Crossroads | 15–18 min | Force choices through mutually exclusive missions |
| Chapter 4: The Weight of North | 8–10 min | Prepare the final decision and deepen ambiguity |
| Finale and ending | 5–7 min | Let physical movement answer the central question |

The game contains ten named mission groups, three mutually exclusive mission pairs, one final free-roam farewell sequence, four core ending directions, and relationship-dependent variants.

## 4. Setting: Greybridge

Greybridge is a fictional present-day North American industrial town. Its steelworks and rail depot once supported the neighborhood, but both have closed. Stores are disappearing, families are moving, and young people commonly assume that a meaningful future exists somewhere else.

Beyond the northern ridge is a larger city whose lights can be seen from an old textile-factory rooftop. The friends have never properly visited it. As children, they began calling the lights simply “North.” North is therefore both a possible destination and a future they invented together.

### 4.1 Primary locations

#### Old Neighborhood

The main exploration area contains Jamie's apartment, Ruth's Diner, Brooks Market, the Vale family electronics shop, Maya's mural wall, a bus stop, a closed railway crossing, and the alley stairs leading to the rooftop.

The neighborhood changes between chapters rather than being replaced by new maps. Clearance signs appear, lights go out, moving boxes accumulate, posters tear, and familiar background characters disappear.

#### Vale Auto Garage

The garage is Elias's workplace and the group's headquarters. The friends are repairing a faded blue station wagon. Their childhood initials remain scratched inside one door. A childhood drawing by Maya and the old rooftop map hang on the wall.

#### Rooftop Overlook

The rooftop contains a rusted folding chair, chalk arrows, initials, a weathered map, and a view of the northern lights. The same composition returns in the prologue, the central rupture, and the epilogue. Changes in attendance and blocking communicate the state of the group.

## 5. Motifs and Their Development

### North

- **Early:** freedom, hope, opportunity, and togetherness.
- **Middle:** a promise that exerts pressure and reduces other possibilities.
- **End:** one valid direction among several, not the correct answer.

### The station wagon

- **Early:** a shared project that gives the group purpose.
- **Middle:** proof of Elias's sacrifices and a source of emotional debt.
- **End:** a working vehicle that cannot repair the group's divergence.

### The rooftop

- **Early:** a place where imagination makes the friends equal.
- **Middle:** a place where incompatible interpretations of the promise surface.
- **End:** a place from which Jamie can see North without being compelled to pursue it.

### Closing businesses

The closures give leaving a legitimate appeal, preventing the narrative from romanticizing stagnation. They also show that staying can require active rebuilding rather than passive comfort.

### Maps and directions

The old map, mission markers, chalk arrow, and unmarked road explore the difference between guidance and ownership. The final unmarked path is visible but never highlighted by the quest interface.

## 6. Characters

### 6.1 Jamie — the player character

**Age:** 17  
**Presentation:** Gender-neutral name and restrained gender expression  
**Surface traits:** Quiet, dependable, observant, a good listener  
**Strength:** Makes other people feel heard  
**Flaw:** Avoids disappointing others by letting the group define the decision  
**Need:** To accept that uncertainty does not invalidate personal agency

Jamie has desires but cannot distinguish them from the group's shared identity. The player constructs Jamie's emerging personality through actions. Dialogue options express tone—commitment, curiosity, uncertainty, or silence—but never appear as moral categories.

Representative response set:

- “I'll be there.”
- “Do you really want this?”
- “I don't know yet.”
- Remain silent.

### 6.2 Elias Vale — the keeper of the promise

**Age:** 20  
**Surface traits:** Charismatic, practical, protective, decisive  
**Strength:** Works hard and does not abandon people  
**Flaw:** Interprets change as betrayal and care as permission to decide for others  
**Fear:** If the trip fails, his sacrifices and identity will lose their meaning

Elias's older brother left Greybridge years earlier and sent occasional postcards describing life elsewhere. Elias converted those fragments into a promise of rescue. He has spent years saving money, acquiring parts, and organizing the trip. He sincerely loves the group; this is why his pressure must remain emotionally credible.

Core line:

> “I'm not forcing anyone. I'm reminding them who they said they wanted to be.”

Jamie owes Elias a personal debt: Elias welcomed Jamie into the group during a lonely period. Elias therefore believes Jamie is the one person who will not leave him.

### 6.3 Maya Chen — the person who permits herself to change

**Age:** 18  
**Surface traits:** Perceptive, dryly funny, apparently self-assured  
**Strength:** Notices changes other people avoid  
**Flaw:** Protects herself from failure by pretending opportunities do not matter  
**Want:** To become an artist  
**Conflict:** She once assumed art required leaving; a local exhibition offers a different path

At twelve, Maya drew the five friends driving north. Elias preserves the drawing as proof of the promise. Maya comes to resent it because it freezes her as her younger self.

Core exchange:

> **Maya:** “I drew that when I was twelve.”  
> **Elias:** “You drew what we all wanted.”  
> **Maya:** “No. I drew what I thought would keep us together.”

### 6.4 Noah Vale — the person who must speak

**Age:** 17  
**Surface traits:** Careful, courteous, technically capable  
**Strength:** Patient and reliable  
**Flaw:** Mistakes silence for peace  
**Family expectation:** Remain in the family electronics shop  
**Personal interest:** Sound design, field recording, and community radio

Noah is Elias's younger cousin. This makes Elias's planning feel familial rather than merely social. Noah's arc is not simply leave versus stay; he must reject both his father's plan and Elias's plan long enough to make his own.

Core line:

> “My dad has a plan for me. Elias has a plan for me. It's strange how everyone's plan sounds like responsibility when they say it out loud.”

### 6.5 Leo Alvarez — the person who admits attachment

**Age:** 18  
**Surface traits:** Sociable, funny, emotionally perceptive  
**Strength:** Relieves tension and cares for people in practical ways  
**Flaw:** Uses humor to avoid honest vulnerability  
**Hidden commitment:** Helps his grandmother and feels responsible for Ruth's Diner and its regulars

Leo talks most loudly about leaving because he fears that wanting to stay will be judged as cowardice.

Core exchange:

> **Jamie:** “You said you hated this place.”  
> **Leo:** “I hate what it does to people.”  
> **Jamie:** “That isn't the same thing.”  
> **Leo:** “Yeah. Took me long enough.”

## 7. Relationship Model

- **Jamie and Elias:** Gratitude and dependence gradually become a question of ownership.
- **Elias and Maya:** Once the group's most imaginative pair; now the strongest ideological conflict.
- **Elias and Noah:** Elias values Noah's skills but mistakes compliance for agreement.
- **Elias and Leo:** Humor masks the fact that both know Leo does not want to leave.
- **Maya and Noah:** Quietly recognize one another's changes before either speaks publicly.
- **Maya and Leo:** Maya sees through Leo's performance but lets him tell the truth himself.
- **Jamie and the three friends:** Optional time and conflicting missions determine whom Jamie truly comes to understand.

There is no explicit romance system. The friend ending represents solidarity and chosen relationship, not selection of a romantic partner.

## 8. Detailed Narrative Flow

### 8.1 Prologue — The Lights North

**Format:** Pre-rendered cinematic, 45 seconds.

At age twelve, the five friends sit on the rooftop during a Greybridge blackout. The distant city lights appear unusually bright.

> **Young Elias:** “See those lights?”  
> **Young Maya:** “That's a water tower.”  
> **Young Elias:** “Tonight, it's north.”  
> **Young Jamie:** “What's there?”  
> **Young Elias:** “Whatever isn't here.”  
> **Young Leo:** “Do they have better fries?”  
> **Young Noah:** “Statistically, probably.”  
> **Young Elias:** “Then it's settled. We save up, fix a car, and we all go.”  
> **Young Maya:** “All of us?”  
> **Young Elias:** “All of us.”

They sign the reverse of an old map. The image cuts to the present-day map pinned inside the garage. A handwritten board reads **FIVE DAYS**.

### 8.2 Chapter 1 — The Plan

**Purpose:** Teach interaction and make the shared plan desirable.

#### Mission 1: Clock In

Jamie works a short shift at Ruth's Diner. The player learns movement, interaction, item carrying, and simple task completion by delivering orders and clearing tables. Earnings enter the shared travel fund.

> **Leo:** “Five more shifts and I retire forever.”  
> **Jamie:** “You've worked here for three weeks.”  
> **Leo:** “Exactly. I've given this place my youth.”

#### Mission 2: The Missing Socket

At the garage, the player finds a missing socket wrench in the back alley and installs the car battery through a short repair interaction. Elias is competent and supportive.

> **Elias:** “Friday. Six in the morning. No speeches, no delays.”  
> **Maya:** “You just gave a speech.”  
> **Elias:** “That was a schedule.”

#### Mission 3: Parts of a Future

Jamie collects a fan belt from Brooks Market, fuses from the electronics shop, and a toolbox from the diner. Each retrieval introduces a location that will later change or close.

#### Mission 4: Rooftop Inventory

The group counts its money and parts. The player can speak to every friend. The mood remains warm.

> **Elias:** “Next week, this place will be behind us.”

The camera holds on a neighborhood sign going dark. The countdown advances to **FOUR DAYS**.

### 8.3 Chapter 2 — Cracks

**World state:** Brooks Market displays **FINAL WEEK**. Moving boxes appear outside several homes.

#### Mission 5: The Last Sign

Maya asks Jamie to help remove the market's old wooden sign for reuse as a painting surface. Jamie discovers a local exhibition invitation. Maya dismisses it.

> **Jamie:** “You didn't tell them.”  
> **Maya:** “There's nothing to tell.”  
> **Jamie:** “They invited you.”  
> **Maya:** “An invitation isn't a future.”

#### Mission 6: Dead Air

Jamie helps Noah repair a recorder through a wiring puzzle. It contains his field recordings: train sounds, the diner bell, garage tools, and rooftop wind.

> **Jamie:** “Why record things you want to leave?”  
> **Noah:** “Because leaving and forgetting aren't the same.”

#### Mission 7: One More Table

Jamie helps Leo return an old table to Ruth's Diner. Optional inspection reveals that it belonged to Leo's grandmother and that Leo's open suitcase contains no clothes.

#### Chapter close

On the rooftop, Maya asks whether the departure must happen Friday. Elias calmly redraws the date in thicker marker.

> **Elias:** “If we keep moving the date, it stops being a plan.”  
> **Maya:** “Maybe plans are allowed to move.”  
> **Elias:** “People say that when they're scared.”

The countdown advances to **THREE DAYS**.

### 8.4 Chapter 3 — Crossroads

**Purpose:** Convert theme into action. This chapter spans Days Three and Two. Three mission pairs appear sequentially. Entering one mission's commitment zone locks the alternative and changes its world state. A short neutral warning—“This will take the rest of the evening”—communicates mechanical consequence without describing morality. After Pair B, the garage countdown visibly advances to **TWO DAYS**, establishing that the final pair occurs the following day.

#### Pair A: Elias or Maya

##### The Alternator

Jamie stays at the garage to install the alternator before dark. Elias discusses his brother.

> **Elias:** “He said the first night away was the first time he could breathe.”  
> **Jamie:** “Does he still call?”  
> **Elias:** “He's busy.”  
> **Jamie:** “That wasn't my question.”

Completion increases car progress and the Promise tendency.

##### First Light

Jamie attends Maya's sparsely attended exhibition. Her paintings depict Greybridge's closures rather than North. The arts coordinator offers continued studio access.

> **Maya:** “I thought painting this place meant I couldn't escape it.”  
> **Jamie:** “And now?”  
> **Maya:** “Now I think leaving is easier than looking at it.”

Completion increases Maya's bond and the Connection tendency.

#### Pair B: Elias or Noah

##### Road Test

Jamie helps Elias test the station wagon. It stalls, and the two push it back together.

> **Elias:** “Everyone loves the dream. Nobody wants the weight.”  
> **Jamie:** “Maybe it isn't their dream anymore.”  
> **Elias:** “Then they should have said that before I carried it for them.”

##### Static

Noah's father discovers his community-radio application. Jamie helps Noah transport his equipment. Noah stops twice, creating opportunities to abandon the task and return; continuing constitutes support through action.

> **Father:** “A hobby doesn't keep the lights on.”  
> **Noah:** “Neither does pretending I chose this.”  
> **Father:** “You're walking away from your family.”  
> **Noah:** “I'm trying to find out who's walking.”

#### Pair C: Elias or Leo

##### Pack the Trunk

Jamie packs a trunk with insufficient capacity. The available items are repair tools, the childhood box, Maya's painting, Noah's recorder, and Leo's travel bag. The player cannot include everything. The selected items affect later visual details, not morality scores.

##### Last Night Open

Ruth's Diner closes early because of rent pressure. Jamie helps Leo serve its final customers and shut down.

> **Leo:** “I kept saying I'd leave first.”  
> **Jamie:** “Why?”  
> **Leo:** “Because if I joked about leaving, nobody could accuse me of being too scared to go.”  
> **Jamie:** “Are you scared?”  
> **Leo:** “Of leaving? Yeah. Of staying? Also yeah. That's the annoying part.”

After the lights go out:

> **Leo:** “Do you think choosing a place means it gets to keep you?”

#### Character highlight cinematic

The strongest completed friend bond selects one of three 45–60 second pre-rendered sequences: Maya's exhibition, Noah's family confrontation, or Leo closing the diner. Ties resolve in favor of the earliest completed friend mission. Non-selected arcs remain understandable through real-time dialogue.

#### Rooftop fracture cinematic

All five gather at the rooftop. Maya declines the Friday departure. Noah asks for time. Leo tries and fails to joke. Elias loses his composure without becoming violent.

> **Elias:** “We made one promise.”  
> **Maya:** “We were twelve.”  
> **Elias:** “We were honest.”  
> **Maya:** “We were scared.”  
> **Elias:** “And now you're all pretending fear is growth.”  
> **Noah:** “Maybe growth is admitting the plan doesn't fit.”  
> **Elias:** “It fit when I was fixing everything.”  
> **Leo:** “That's the problem, Eli. You started fixing us too.”

Elias leaves. Jamie remains beside the faded chalk arrow. The countdown advances to **ONE DAY**.

### 8.5 Chapter 4 — The Weight of North

**World state:** The market and diner are dark. The North poster is torn. The car runs. Streets are quieter.

#### Mission 8: Things We Leave

Jamie packs at home. The player may inspect all objects but carry only one:

- Group photograph — continuity with the shared past.
- Blank notebook — openness to uncertainty.
- House key — continuing connection to home.
- Old map — continued respect for the original direction.

The game does not explain the symbolism. The selected object reappears in the ending.

#### Mission 9: The Spare Key

Jamie finds two keys at the garage rather than five. Elias has privately accepted that only Jamie may accompany him.

> **Jamie:** “Why only two?”  
> **Elias:** “Because I'm done begging people to want their own future.”  
> **Jamie:** “Their future—or yours?”  
> **Elias:** “You really sound like them now.”

Jamie may continue the conversation or leave silently.

#### Mission 10: Before Morning

The player has a final free-roam period and may visit all three friends.

> **Maya:** “If you go, don't go because he remembers an older version of you.”

> **Noah:** “I kept waiting to feel certain. I don't think certainty comes first.”

> **Leo:** “Whatever you do, don't turn it into proof that the rest of us were wrong.”

Visiting a friend does not commit Jamie to that ending.

### 8.6 Finale — Are You Coming?

**Format:** Pre-rendered cinematic transitioning seamlessly to real-time control.

At dawn, Elias starts the station wagon and places the second key on its roof. Maya, Noah, and Leo occupy separate edges of the playable scene. Maya stands toward the arts center, Noah between the shop and radio station, and Leo beside the closed diner. An unmarked road leads away from both North and the neighborhood.

Elias asks only:

> “Are you coming?”

Control returns. There is no objective text, choice menu, timer, highlighted destination, or explanatory narration. Music recedes until wind and engine noise dominate. Each endpoint uses a generous trigger zone and a short hold-to-commit pause so accidental movement cannot select an ending.

## 9. Ending Design

### 9.1 Northbound — toward the car

Jamie takes the key and enters the car.

High Promise variant:

> **Elias:** “Ready?”  
> **Jamie:** “No.”  
> **Elias:** “Good. Me neither.”

Low Promise variant:

> **Elias:** “I knew you'd remember.”

The second line should feel both loving and unsettling. The game never shows whether North proves successful.

End card:

> Some promises carry us forward. Some ask us how long we are willing to be carried.

### 9.2 Home, Chosen — back into the neighborhood

With high Connection, Jamie turns on a light in the garage or former diner, suggesting active rebuilding. With low Connection, Jamie sits at the bus stop without a complete plan. Both remain legitimate choices.

End card:

> Staying is not the absence of a journey when staying is a choice.

### 9.3 No Map — toward the unmarked road

Jamie leaves alone in a direction not previously named. The carried object changes the final action. With the notebook, Jamie writes the date. With the map, Jamie folds it and keeps it rather than destroying it.

End card:

> Not every road begins with a destination.

### 9.4 Not Alone — toward a friend

The selected friend is the one Jamie physically approaches. All three friend endings are available in every playthrough; level blocking keeps their paths and trigger zones visually separate.

#### Maya variant

Jamie helps paint a new mural over—but not completely erasing—the old North arrow.

> **Maya:** “Do you know what comes next?”  
> **Jamie:** “No.”  
> **Maya:** “Good. Leave me some space.”

#### Noah variant

Jamie enters the temporary radio booth. Noah offers headphones playing Greybridge's collected sounds.

> **Noah:** “This isn't a plan.”  
> **Jamie:** “It's a start.”

#### Leo variant

Jamie helps light the diner. Leo turns over the CLOSED sign; the reverse side is blank.

> **Leo:** “We should probably decide what this place is.”  
> **Jamie:** “Tomorrow.”

Shared end card:

> A life can be chosen by direction—and by who we choose to meet there.

## 10. Choice and State Model

The game tracks four invisible dimensions:

- **Promise:** Completing car and Elias missions.
- **Connection:** Supporting friends, community, and local spaces.
- **Self-direction:** Exploring unmarked spaces, questioning assumptions, and tolerating uncertainty.
- **Bonds:** Separate Maya, Noah, and Leo relationship values.

These values alter tone and detail but never override the player's final physical direction. Specifically, they may change:

- Elias's final line.
- The selected character-highlight cinematic.
- Each friend's posture, prop, and optional line at dawn; all three friends remain present and approachable.
- Optional farewell lines.
- Props visible in the car or final scene.
- Jamie's carried object and final gesture.
- Environmental lighting in Home, Chosen.

The interface never displays morality, approval, alignment, or ending progress. Choices are stored as named narrative facts rather than exposed numeric scores wherever practical—for example, `attended_maya_exhibition`, `completed_road_test`, and `packed_noah_recorder`.

## 11. Interaction Design

The experience contains no combat. Interaction supports character and theme:

- Diner order delivery teaches movement and observation.
- Car repair interactions make the shared promise physically laborious.
- The recorder wiring puzzle reveals Noah's private interest.
- Exhibition setup reveals Maya through composition and object placement.
- Trunk packing makes limited capacity a metaphor without explanatory text.
- Environmental inspection rewards attention with context, not currency.
- Optional rooftop conversations allow quiet pacing.
- Unmarked alleys and objects support self-directed exploration.

No interaction should exceed approximately 90 seconds or require repeated failure. Failure resets the local interaction without losing narrative progress. Accessibility settings should allow timed interactions to be slowed or skipped.

## 12. Dialogue Rules

1. Characters do not say “Design My Life,” “choose your own path,” “follow your dream,” or “there is no correct answer.”
2. Every major character must be both reasonable and flawed.
3. Characters speak about immediate concerns—money, schedules, family, the car, work, and friendship—rather than explaining the theme.
4. Dialogue options express Jamie's tone, not moral categories.
5. Silence is a valid response but never a universally superior one.
6. Important facts are repeated through environment or action so skipping optional dialogue does not make the plot incomprehensible.
7. Ordinary gameplay dialogue appears as English text with portraits and short vocal reactions. Cinematics use full English voiceover and English subtitles.
8. All voiced lines must also have subtitles; subtitle size and background opacity are configurable.

## 13. Cinematic Plan

| Sequence | Duration | Function |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Opening Promise | 40–50 sec | Establish shared history and visual motif |
| Character Highlight | 45–60 sec | Humanize the strongest supported friend arc; one of three produced variants plays |
| Rooftop Fracture | 60–75 sec | Break the group's unified interpretation |
| Are You Coming? | 30–45 sec | Stage the final question and return agency |

The four playback slots require six produced video files: one Opening Promise, three Character Highlight variants, one Rooftop Fracture, and one Are You Coming? clip. All cinematics must:

- Match character clothing, palette, and silhouettes used in gameplay.
- Use English voice and subtitles.
- Be skippable after the first two seconds.
- Apply their narrative state even when skipped.
- Restore player control, audio mix, camera, and input correctly.
- Avoid containing choices that would desynchronize the pre-rendered image from gameplay state.

The endings are rendered in-engine to preserve continuity with the player's physical choice and selected props.

## 14. Visual and Audio Direction

Gameplay uses a stylized 2.5D presentation: painted textures, readable silhouettes, modest depth, long evening shadows, and restrained animation. The camera remains top-down at a diagonal angle. Cinematics use stylized 3D rather than photorealism so the transition does not feel like a different story universe.

The palette moves from warm amber and blue in Chapter 1 toward cooler, emptier mornings and reduced storefront lighting. North remains visually attractive throughout; the game must not make it obviously sinister.

Audio carries memory and place. Recurring sounds include the diner bell, distant train, garage tools, rooftop wind, radio static, and the station wagon's engine. Noah's recordings allow these sounds to become narrative objects. The final scene removes most music so the player's movement feels exposed and deliberate.

## 15. Narrative Failure Handling

- A missed optional interaction never blocks chapter completion.
- If the player leaves a conflicting mission before commitment, both alternatives remain available.
- After commitment, the other mission updates to a believable missed state rather than disappearing without explanation.
- Skipped cinematics set the same completion facts as watched cinematics.
- Saving during a cinematic resumes before the cinematic rather than inside it.
- Ending triggers require a brief confirmation hold through continued movement, preventing accidental selection.
- If relationship values tie, deterministic rules choose the earliest completed bond for the highlight cinematic; the final physical friend choice remains unrestricted.

## 16. Acceptance Criteria

The narrative implementation is complete when:

1. A new player can finish one coherent path in 45–60 minutes.
2. All required plot facts remain understandable after skipping optional dialogue.
3. Each of the three mission pairs permits only one completed alternative.
4. The neighborhood visibly changes at every chapter transition.
5. All four core ending directions are reachable without hidden unlock requirements.
6. Maya, Noah, and Leo variants of Not Alone are individually reachable.
7. Hidden tendencies change ending details but never choose the ending for the player.
8. Elias remains sympathetic in playtest feedback and is not broadly read as a simple villain.
9. Playtesters can describe the central theme in their own words without the game stating it.
10. No interface labels an action or ending as morally correct.
11. Cinematics may be skipped without breaking mission, audio, camera, or input state.
12. English dialogue and subtitles remain legible at the target display resolution.

## 17. Out of Scope for the First Complete Version

- Combat, weapons, or enemy AI.
- Open-world driving.
- Online multiplayer or accounts.
- Full voice acting for ordinary dialogue.
- Procedural quests.
- More than the three primary map locations.
- Romance routes.
- A fifth “true” ending.
- A large inventory or economy system.
- Photorealistic character models.

These exclusions protect the 45–60 minute narrative experience and keep the project feasible while preserving the approved theme.
